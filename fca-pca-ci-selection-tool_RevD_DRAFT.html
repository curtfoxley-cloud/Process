Designate this CI for audit?

CM answers every question here. CM answers from documentation. Where the documentation is unclear, the answer is open and CM escalates to the function that owns it.

Candidate CI
How to answer

Read the documentation first. Each question names the documentation set the answer is derived from, and the function that owns it. The owning function supplies the documentation. It does not supply the answer.

An answer that has not been established from its source is not a NO. Mark it open, name the owner, set a date, and escalate. The assessment is not signed until every open answer resolves.

When to run this assessment

Complete one assessment per CI at initial audit planning, at each CM-plan revision, and on any reassessment trigger. The reassessment triggers are a substantive design, process, facility, tooling, or supplier change, which is a safety management system management-of-change trigger; a new quality escape or safety event traceable to the CI; a CSA reconciliation anomaly; a hazard log revision adding a risk control to the CI; and a sub-supplier effectiveness determination of less than effective for a supplier building the CI. Record the result and rationale for every CI evaluated, designated for audit or not. Attach it to the Audit Designation Assessment Record.

A saved file spares you retyping. It is not the record, and it is not a tracked action. An open answer is an assigned action with an owner and a date, and it belongs in whatever tracks those.

General Notes

CM answers. The functions supply. CM derives every answer from a documentation set and records the source relied on. Systems Engineering and Design Engineering jointly supply the design history and the released design definition, and author the technical content of each engineering change. Airworthiness and Certification supplies the certification basis, the previously approved data, and the change classification where the program has a certification basis. System Safety supplies the hazard log, the safety analyses, and the severities. RM&SH owns and supplies the FMECA and the single point failure identification. Quality supplies the escape, nonconformance, and foreign object findings. Supplier Requirements Engineering supplies the procurement records and the supplier classification log. CM supplies the item index, the change records against the CI since its last audit, the CSA reconciliation indicators, the prior audit records, and the supplier effectiveness determinations.

Escalate on unclear documentation. CM opens the FMECA to see whether the item is associated with a safety-related function. If the FMECA answers, that is the answer and the FMECA is the source. If the FMECA is unclear, CM asks RM&SH. That order holds for all sixteen questions.

A no that means nobody checked is the most expensive answer on the form. It reads exactly like a no that somebody established, and only one of them is true.

Signatures confirm. They do not determine. CM and Systems Engineering sign. System Safety signs whenever any safety question was answered yes. A concurrence signature confirms that the technical basis is sound. The determination is CM's.

Audit scope. Designation says whether the CI is audited. Scope says which audit. Both are read from the same answers. The FCA asks whether the design was verified against its requirements. The PCA asks whether the unit conforms to its product definition. An answer that raises doubt about verification points to an FCA. An answer that raises doubt about manufacture, sources, or records points to a PCA. The tool recommends a scope from the answers. The recommendation is not a substitute for the contract, which sets a minimum that the assessment may expand and may not reduce.

A PCA on its own needs a completed FCA. A PCA does not begin unless the corresponding FCA has been successfully completed or is being conducted concurrently, because the PCA examines the as-built configuration of a validated CI. Where the CI has never been validated and the answers point to a PCA, designate both.

Part A is contractual. A contract, SOW, or CMP call-out makes designation mandatory. Part B and Part C still run. Contract designation sets a minimum on scope, not a ceiling. Stopping at Part A is the most common error on this assessment.

Part B is absolute. Any yes in Part B designates the CI for audit, for both an FCA and a PCA. There is no risk factor threshold to reach and no concurrence route by which that designation is set aside. Conducting the FCA and conducting the PCA are themselves entries in the register of safety-mandatory CM requirements, so a proposal not to conduct an audit the CI is designated for goes to the safety disposition gate: performed, replaced with an independently validated equivalent control, or formally risk-accepted at the authority matching the recorded severity. System Safety signs the assessment. System Safety does not concur in the omission.

Part C is cumulative. One risk factor is recorded and monitored. Two risk factors designate the CI, or the program documents the rationale and CM and Systems Engineering sign it.

Ceremony versus substance. An internally initiated audit may tailor the ceremony. It may not tailor the substance. The safety-mandatory reconciliation, the traceability matrix, and the as-built examination are performed in full.

Glossary. CSA is Configuration Status Accounting. PBL is the Product Baseline. FMECA is the Failure Modes, Effects, and Criticality Analysis. FOD is Foreign Object Debris. RFA is a Request for Action. CSCI is a Computer Software Configuration Item. RM&SH is Reliability, Maintainability, Safety, and Health.

Reference. Configuration Audit Process (FCA/PCA), §5.2.1 Audit Designation, and Appendix C, the FCA/PCA CI Selection Checklist.
